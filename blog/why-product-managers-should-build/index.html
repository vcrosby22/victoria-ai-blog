Why Product Managers Should Build Things

Product management frameworks are important. But nothing teaches product sense like actually shipping software — even if it's rough.

#product-management#building#shipping#craft

There’s a version of product management that lives entirely in documents, dashboards, and stakeholder meetings. It’s a valid career path. But I think it leaves something on the table.

The gap between specification and reality

When you’ve never built software yourself, there’s an invisible gap between what you think you’re asking for and what you’re actually asking for. You write a spec. Engineering interprets it. Something ships. It’s close, but not quite what you imagined — and you can’t always articulate why.

Building things yourself — even small, rough things — collapses that gap. You start to feel the difference between “simple” and “easy.” You develop intuition for what’s a one-hour ask versus a one-week ask.

AI makes this accessible

This is the part that’s changed dramatically. You don’t need to become a software engineer. With AI coding agents, someone with product sense and basic technical literacy can ship real software. Not prototypes — actual working tools.

I’ve built:

  • A financial monitoring agent that pulls market data and generates reports
  • A personal knowledge management system
  • Static sites and reports deployed to production

None of these required me to be an expert developer. They required me to:

  1. Know what I wanted
  2. Be willing to iterate
  3. Understand enough to evaluate what the agent produced

What you learn by building

  • Scope becomes visceral — you feel scope creep in your bones when you’re the one implementing it
  • Trade-offs become real — “let’s just add one more field” stops being free
  • Empathy deepens — you understand why engineers push back on certain requests
  • Speed of learning accelerates — reading about something vs. building it are different orders of magnitude

Start small

You don’t need to build the next SaaS product. Start with a tool that solves a problem you actually have. A script that automates a workflow. A dashboard for data you check manually. A blog.

The point isn’t the artifact. The point is what building teaches you about products.
