Redesigning a Gallery Site When the Platform Isn't the Problem

Susan Street Fine Art's site had 150 images and almost nothing to do on the page. The fix was 80% content and design — and a POC that proved 13× lighter performance without leaving Artlogic.

#redesign#gallery#performance#artlogic#inquiry-driven

Susan Street Fine Art has been on Cedros Avenue in Solana Beach for decades. The gallery sells relationships and provenance — inquiry-driven, not e-commerce. Collectors browse, they inquire, they visit.

The website didn’t communicate any of that.

When I tore it down against our design knowledge base — information architecture, static-site patterns, UI principles — the diagnosis was blunt: the site is doing one job (showing some imagery) and skipping every job that converts a visitor into an inquiry or a gallery visit.

I built a proof-of-concept redesign at ssfa-redesign-poc.pages.dev to show what “serious gallery” looks like on the same business model, with measured performance wins.

What’s actually on susanstreet.com today

  • Header: logo + four links — Artists, Portfolio, Consulting, Contact. No phone, no “Inquire,” no location.
  • Body: a single auto-rotating image banner. Six slides. No headline, no value proposition, no current exhibition, no text in the main content area.
  • Footer: address, phone, email, two marketplace links.

First-time visitors cannot tell what the gallery stands for, who it represents, or what to do next. Top collector tasks — see the current show, browse an artist, inquire about a work, plan a visit — aren’t reachable from the header.

Regen Projects runs the same legacy Artlogic “exhibit-E” engine and looks world-class. Platform isn’t the ceiling. Content and design are.

Headline recommendation: upgrade to modern Artlogic Website (Susan Street is already on Artlogic’s stack) and rebuild around an exhibition-led, inquiry-driven model — “Now on View” first, artwork detail pages with Inquire CTAs, a proper Consulting offer, and confident Solana Beach identity.

Squarespace or Shopify would be the wrong move. This is not a checkout business.

What good looks like (and who to steal from)

Blue-chip galleries (Gagosian, David Zwirner, Pace): exhibition-led homepage, editorial authority layer, location as prestige, inquiry not checkout, radical restraint. The work is the UI.

Local competitors: Madison Gallery in Solana Beach (same town) runs an exhibition reel homepage — current, upcoming, past, every entry dated — plus explicit collector services and regional authority framing. Quint Gallery in La Jolla proves a small program can look serious with very little chrome.

Not the model: Saatchi Art’s commerce-first IA — priced grids, checkout, impulse buying. Susan Street sells relationships. Borrow browse and trust cues; reject the cart.

Why the images feel like the 1990s (measured)

I inspected the live markup, headers, and actual downloads. Five compounding causes:

FindingImpact
~150 separate image requests on one project pageBrowser queues connections; trickle load
A 236px thumbnail weighs 194 KBShould be ~15–30 KB
No modern format — CDN returns JPEG even when browser asks for AVIF/WebPLegacy URLs omit f_auto/q_auto flags
No effective lazy-loading — thumbnails carry direct srcAll 150 download on page load
CloudFront cache misses on first visitOrigin generates many derivatives on demand

One project page: ~28 MB. No wonder it “takes a minute.”

The good news: the same CDN serves optimized WebP the moment you add f_auto,q_auto to the URL. Combined with lazy-loading and fewer images per view, the win is dramatic without leaving Artlogic.

The POC: same art, 13× lighter

I pulled Susan Street’s actual project and artwork images, converted them to right-sized WebP, and built three demo pages — Home, Artists, Projects.

Entire image set for all three pages: ~1.0 MB total vs ~28 MB for a single current project page. Same art. Lazy-loaded. Native loading="lazy". Exhibition-first IA with Inquire CTAs, consulting offer, and Solana Beach identity.

Live preview: ssfa-redesign-poc.pages.dev

The POC isn’t a platform migration pitch. It’s proof that 80% content/design, 20% platform — the IA rebuild and image discipline matter more than ripping out Artlogic.

Design decisions in the POC

  • “Now on View” above the fold — time-relevant programming so the site reads alive
  • One primary CTA per view — Inquire, not five competing actions
  • Artwork detail pages — medium, dimensions, advisor path; no prices (inquiry model)
  • Consulting as a first-class offer — in-home curatorial services, collection development, on-site consultation
  • Accessibility baseline — meaningful alt text on hero and artwork images (the live site ships empty alt='' on carousel slides)
  • Mobile tasks, not mobile slideshow — something to do on a phone, not just pinch-zoom a banner

What I’d do differently

I’d measure Core Web Vitals on the live site before writing recommendations — the image audit was enough to make the case, but LCP numbers on the POC vs production would strengthen a client presentation.

I’d also scope the Artlogic migration path explicitly: exhibit-E → modern Artlogic Website is a vendor conversation, not a weekend rebuild. The POC validates direction; migration is a project with its own timeline and content migration checklist.

Inquiry-driven sites share DNA with nonprofit builds — clear CTAs, trust before conversion, integrations behind the scenes. See Research Before Pixels for the nonprofit side of that pattern.
