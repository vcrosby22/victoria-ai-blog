Proof Over Pitch: Why I Ship in Public

Resumes claim competence. URLs prove you can ship. Here is how I use live products, open writing, and a public knowledge base as one coordinated marketing engine — without pretending I have a growth team.

#personal-brand#marketing#shipping#portfolio#ai-assisted-work#pm

I am a product and program leader who also builds. That combination is common on LinkedIn and rare in receipts. Anyone can list “AI” on a profile. Fewer people can point to a live system and walk you through the architecture, tradeoffs, and the week it almost broke in production.

So I stopped treating side projects as hobbies and started treating them as marketing infrastructure — not in a gross growth-hack way, but in a simple go-to-market way:

Proof beats pitch.

The asset stack I want people to find

When someone evaluates whether to work with you — hiring manager, collaborator, client, conference organizer — they are running a fast heuristic: is this person serious, and can they finish?

I want that heuristic to land on yes before we ever get on a call. The stack, for me, looks like this:

  1. A live product with a URL. My daily financial report is not a slide deck. It is a pipeline: ingestion, scoring, narrative analysis, HTML generation, CI, GitHub Pages. You can disagree with the model. You cannot pretend I only talk about delivery.

  2. Writing that shows how I think. This blog exists because explanations travel farther than private notes. Posts are long enough to expose reasoning — tradeoffs, mistakes, and constraints — not just victory laps.

  3. A public knowledge base for depth. AI Assisted Work is the “if you want the syllabus” layer: onboarding paths, context engineering notes, and curated references for readers who are not only browsing on a lunch break.

None of these replace a conversation. They compress the conversation. By the time someone emails me, I want the first thirty minutes of small talk about “do you actually build?” already finished.

Positioning without a positioning workshop

Classic marketing advice says: pick an ICP (ideal customer profile), message map, differentiation. I still think that advice is good when you are selling a SKU to a known buyer.

For an individual builder–PM brand, I run a lighter version:

  • Primary audience: people who hire or partner with technical PMs and hybrid ICs — the ones who care about judgment and execution.
  • Secondary audience: practitioners who want to steal workflows — Cursor habits, risk framing, how I use agents without handing over the steering wheel.
  • Proof language: ship dates, public repos, live pages, and honest “pain points left to solve” sections. Credibility lives next to the blemishes.

The through-line is not “I am the best.” It is “here is the body of work; here is what I would do next if this were a funded product.”

How AI changes the economics of this stack

Tools like Cursor do not replace taste. They lower the fixed cost of turning an idea into something URL-shaped. That matters for marketing because surface area compounds:

  • A report that used to be a weekend sketch can become a maintained product with tests and CI guardrails.
  • A blog post can be drafted, challenged, and tightened with a sparring partner that never sleeps — still my call on what ships.

The marketing win is not “I used AI.” Everyone will say that. The win is cadence plus quality: more shipped artifacts per quarter, each one pointing at the same narrative — I build end-to-end systems and I can explain them.

What I am deliberately not doing

  • I am not buying ads to funnel strangers into a lead form. That may come later; it is not the spine of the story today.
  • I am not inventing metrics I cannot defend. The point is inspectable truth: a link, a commit history, a risk score that updates when the world changes.

If you want to try this without a rebrand

You do not need a blog empire. You need one credible artifact you would happily put in the first line of an email — something a stranger can click and understand in two minutes. Build that, write the README or launch post, and repeat once you still like the first one a month later.

If you are already shipping in private, ask whether the next iteration can be public by default. That single habit moves you from telling a story about your skills to exhibiting them.
